* Research question and owner
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-evidence-question
:END:

How can one engine retain the difference between finding evidence, using it
for a claim, checking that use, and propagating a changed premise through
derived artifacts?  Search, a short answer, an extended task and autonomous
research all instantiate these relations.  Their task labels and activity
types are *not* public engine primitives.  =evidence/= owns immutable
POO-native relation values and pure graph queries; =revisions/= owns
admission/valid-time evidence history; =truth-maintenance/= owns cut-bound
reevaluation scheduling.  A runtime or research Provider owns retrieval,
source inspection, semantic checking and publication effects.

The present relation model does not infer that a retrieved document is
relevant, that an assessment is correct, that a claim is true, or that a
reachable downstream claim actually changed.  Those are separate,
evidence-bearing judgments.

* Semantic contract
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-evidence-contract
:END:

Let =Q= name queries or research questions, =E= immutable evidence versions,
=C= claims or artifact nodes, and =R ⊆ Q×E= be retrieval receipts.  Let
=U ⊆ E×C= be declared uses, each binding a source digest and *claim
content digest*.  Let =A ⊆ U×Assessor×Method×Verdict×Basis= be separately
supplied assessments and =D ⊆ C×C= be declared derivation edges.  Verdicts
are =supports=, =refutes= or =uncertain=.  Every assessment binds the exact
semantic digest of an existing use; revising a source or claim cannot reuse
the former assessment.  Evidence identity maps to one source digest and
claim identity to one claim digest in a lineage.  A changed version requires
a new identity or an explicit revision.  Relations and
lineage are immutable and content-bound.  The ordinary construction path is
POO objects, not raw tuple lists.

#+name: temporal-evidence-judgment-levels
#+caption: Do not promote a weaker statement to a stronger one.
| Level | Required evidence | What the current module says |
|-------+-------------------+------------------------------|
| Retrieved | =R(q,e)= and source digest | A candidate source was recorded for a question. |
| Used | =U(e,c)= with source and claim digests | A particular claim version cites or relies on a particular evidence version. |
| Attested | Separate =A(u,assessor,method,verdict,basis)= | A named judgment was supplied for that exact use; its content and independence are not verified here. |
| Admitted claim | Verified assessment policy, version-consistent proof and runtime selection | Outside this module. |
| Actual impact | Recomputed downstream results with semantic comparison and causal assumptions where needed | Outside this module; reachability is only a potential impact frontier. |

For changed evidence =e=, the potential frontier is the least fixed point

\[F_e = \mu X.\bigl(\{c\mid U(e,c)\}\cup
                     \{c'\mid \exists c\in X:D(c,c')\}\bigr).\]

The Scheme implementation builds reverse claim dependencies and visits each
reachable claim at most once, so cycles terminate.  =F_e= is relative to
the supplied edge inventory.  Missing uses or derivation edges make the
frontier incomplete; this module does not assert global dependency
completeness.  The use audit reports retrieved-but-unused evidence,
cited-but-unretrieved evidence, unreviewed uses, attested support,
refutation, uncertainty and conflict.  If at least one assessment supports
and another refutes the same use, it is =contested=; uncertainty also blocks
the aggregate =declared-reviewed= status.  This is a classification of
attestation records, not a vote on truth.
An empty bucket is a statement about the supplied inventory only.  An unused
retrieval is a normal exploratory outcome and does not alone make the
aggregate audit =needs-review=; it stays visible for a caller's relevance or
stopping policy.

* Quint behavioral model
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-evidence-quint
:END:

=packages/proofs/quint/EvidenceSemantics.qnt= defines the pure finite candidate-use and
dependency relations and their bounded closure.  The internal relation in
=packages/proofs/quint/EvidenceLineage.qnt= permits any enabled order of retrieval,
citation, use checking, claim admission and evidence correction.  The
finite fixture is =packages/proofs/quint/EvidenceLineageCase.qnt=.  This =Next=-equivalent
exploration relation is a model-checker mechanism, not the public POO API or
a prediction of the model's next action.

The fixture contains two candidate evidence-to-finding uses and a report
dependent on the finding.  The initial relation =soundUses= ranges over
*every subset* of those candidate uses, so Quint checks four possible
validity worlds as well as their operation interleavings.  A checked use may support
admission only while its source has not been corrected.  Citation added
*after* admission invalidates the cited claim and its descendants, because
the old verification did not cover the expanded evidence set.  Correction
invalidates checked uses of the corrected evidence and every active claim
reachable from a *cited* use of it.  Candidate-but-never-cited evidence does
not invalidate a claim.  Quint exposed both rules while exploring orderings;
they are explicit transitions now.

#+name: temporal-evidence-quint-properties
#+caption: Finite safety obligations checked by Quint for the declared fixture.
| Property | Meaning |
|----------+---------|
| =TypeInvariant= | State sets remain inside finite declared universes. |
| =UsePartitionInvariant= | One use is not both checked and rejected. |
| =CheckedUseInvariant= | Every checked use belongs to the selected possible world's sound-use relation and has an uncorrected source. |
| =ActiveSupportInvariant= | Every active claim has checked local use or an active declared parent; cited local uses are all checked. |
| =CorrectionInvariant= | No active claim lies in the cited-use dependency closure of corrected evidence. |

The checker assumes a perfect use-checking oracle *within each possible
world*.  The second finite model,
=packages/proofs/quint/EvidenceAssessment.qnt=, explicitly permits two reviewers to
produce any of =supports/refutes/uncertain= in either order.  A new review
invalidates the cited claim and its descendants before any fresh admission;
source withdrawal does the same.  Its safety properties retain an active
claim only while every cited use has a non-conflicted support classification
or an active declared parent.  This checks the lifecycle of *attestations*,
including disagreement, without treating reviewers as perfect.  The
production system cannot know soundness from a string identity or digest;
evaluator error and correlated assessment failures remain open.
The model checks atomic invalidation; actual concurrent runtime publication
would require a corresponding atomic state transition or versioned CAS and a
refinement argument.  No general Scheme-to-Quint correspondence theorem,
liveness guarantee, fairness assumption, complete source universe, or claim
truth theorem is asserted.

** Candidate exchange with GQL and ASCENT
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-evidence-candidate-exchange
:END:

The [[file:../../../docs/10-19-design/10.06-poo-module-system/161-temporal-causality-and-causal-trajectory-architecture.org::#temporal-candidate-exchange][architecture contract]] places a finite candidate exchange at the
Temporal boundary.  GQL locates source and path candidates; ASCENT derives
candidate relations over a frozen finite snapshot; Temporal retains source
versions, verification, revision and conclusion admission.  A GQL
=SourceQueryReceipt= already records parser/source identity, result digest,
count and completeness.  It has no shared row value that can yet be checked
against ASCENT output.  An adapter must bind materialized rows to the result
digest and the exact Temporal cut before either engine's candidates can be
joined.  No such adapter is claimed by this model.

The abstract =packages/proofs/quint/CandidateExchange.qnt= tests a necessary safety
condition for that adapter: candidate identities may be reported in either
order, with complete or incomplete receipts and on old or current revision
generations.  Each candidate declares a nonempty required-provider set;
a publication attempt requires current-generation complete receipts from
every provider in that set.  A generation revision atomically
clears publication, while old receipts remain visible as historical data.
One candidate/provider/generation has one immutable receipt in the model;
a retry with different completeness needs a new generation or receipt
identity in a richer protocol.

#+name: temporal-candidate-exchange-properties
#+caption: Scope of the finite candidate exchange model.
| Property | Meaning | Excluded obligation |
|----------+---------+---------------------|
| =TypeInvariant= | Finite receipt and publication sets stay within declared universes. | No parser or relation evaluation. |
| =ReceiptUniquenessInvariant= | One candidate/provider/generation does not acquire conflicting completion records. | No source row authentication. |
| =PublicationInvariant= | Published candidates have complete receipts from their declared providers in the current generation. | No truth, causal admission, or real runtime atomicity. |

The Quint fixture has two abstract candidate identities and two generations:
one candidate requires both sources, the other only ASCENT.
It explores interleavings and stale completions; it does not prove the
Scheme runtime refines these transitions.  Candidate agreement is only a
reason to inspect provenance and evidence use, not a semantic verdict.

** Why/Why-Not provenance ownership
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-evidence-provenance-owner
:END:

The term /proof chain/ has two different meanings here.  A *deductive
derivation* explains how a result follows from a fixed finite input
relation and a fixed program.  An *admission justification* explains why
the inputs and their interpretations are acceptable for a particular
Temporal conclusion.  Conflating them would let a correct derivation from
untrusted or obsolete premises masquerade as an accepted conclusion.

#+name: temporal-proof-chain-owner
#+caption: Owner and minimum checkable evidence for each proof-chain layer.
| Layer | Semantic owner | Minimum receipt | Current status |
|-------+----------------+-----------------+----------------|
| GQL query/path | GQL Query Provider | Query and parser identity, graph revision, materialized result-row digest and bounded witness | Current receipt has digest/count/completeness but no shared materialized row contract. |
| ASCENT Why | ASCENT evaluator | Program/rule identity, input fact identities, derivation nodes, exact snapshot, completion and budget | Local ASCENT commit =5fbaba6= adds independently replayable positive atom-rule DAGs with bounded status; non-positive rules remain unsupported. |
| ASCENT Why-Not | ASCENT evaluator under a declared finite domain | Ground query tuple, source/candidate facts, rule-closed relation set, exact snapshot/program identity and result status | ASCENT local commit =6993dc8= generates and independently checks bounded positive-rule nonmembership certificates; non-positive and non-ground cases remain unsupported. |
| Premise meaning and admission | POO Flow Temporal | Evidence cut, source revision, assessment of exact use, authority policy and selected conclusion revision | =candidates/= now binds declared cut/provider/check digests with an inert reviewability audit; no cross-engine row binding or Temporal proof-admission adapter exists. |
| Lifecycle safety | Quint projection owned by Temporal | Generation, required-provider set, complete receipt, atomic invalidation | Finite abstract protocol checked; no runtime refinement proof. |

Positive relational provenance can be represented algebraically over
annotated inputs as in Green, Karvounarakis and Tannen's
[[https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf][provenance semirings]].  Why-Not is a separate semantic problem: a
missing tuple requires a query, a candidate domain and an input-completeness
assumption, as illustrated by [[https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2655][first-order provenance games]].  An
incomplete search or a budget-exhausted closure yields =unknown= or
=bounded=, never an unconditional proof of absence.

Putting the rule-level proof DAG into Temporal would duplicate ASCENT's
evaluator and make Temporal responsible for its fixpoint and update
semantics.  Putting Temporal admission into ASCENT would mistake a valid
deduction for source trust, citation support, user authority or current
revision.  The shared contract therefore carries *references* across the
boundary, while the component that knows each semantics produces and
checks its own evidence.  A source correction retires the old Temporal
scope and forces any ASCENT proof bound to that scope to be re-evaluated;
it does not erase the historical explanation.

The new ASCENT =candidate/provenance.ss= API produces =complete=, =bounded=
or =unsupported= independently of the native query status.  Its positive
proof records source, hypothetical candidate and rule nodes with ordered
input-node identities, query roots, snapshot identity/generation/digest,
candidate digest and work budget.  =candidate-verify-positive-proof=
replays the nodes against the inspected program and native rows.  The
normal =reasoning-attempt= receipt has a separate =proof= field and an
optional proof-work budget.  It accepts multi-relation joins and recursion
within the pure positive atom subset.  Empty results and rules using
negation, =where=, =compute= or =reduce= remain =unsupported= for this
generic proof, even if the native query itself completed.  The earlier
special graph witness/cut remains a separate narrow evidence kind.

The ASCENT positive Why change =5fbaba6= was sent to PR #1 by its owning
thread.  Its later local =6993dc8= adds finite positive-rule Why-Not,
independent verification and bounded test progress; that commit had not
been pushed when this record was written.  =just build= and the 29-module
local Scheme qualification passed.  The abstract Lean theorem and finite
Quint session model passed their focused gate.  Remote CI remains a separate
gate.  These checks do not establish cross-system integration.  Temporal
must still bind
the exact evidence cut to the ASCENT snapshot, require both native and
proof status =complete=, replay the proof, and inspect source assessments
and authority before admitting a conclusion.  A locally implemented
positive-rule Why-Not certificate is described below; non-positive rule
proof and a cross-GQL row/authority adapter remain open.  The
[[file:../candidates/model.org][candidate exchange model]] now records the exact cut,
provider receipt and separate check as POO values, with =reviewable=
explicitly distinct from admission.

*** Conditional Why-Not certificate contract
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-why-not-certificate
:END:

For a fixed finite relation snapshot =D=, candidate facts =H= and
positive rule program =P=, let =T_P= be its immediate-consequence
operator.  A ground target tuple =q= is absent *relative to that exact
program and snapshot* if the evaluator completes and computes a finite
closed set =C= such that

\[D\cup H\subseteq C,\qquad T_P(C)\subseteq C,\qquad q\notin C.\]

By monotonicity, the least fixed point is contained in =C=, so =q= is not
derivable from those premises.  An independent verifier must replay
source/candidate rows, every supported rule instance or a complete rule
closure check, the ground query and the source/program digests.  The
certificate is invalidated for current use when the Temporal evidence cut
or program changes, while remaining a historical result for the old cut.
This is the mathematical contract for ASCENT's separate, locally
implemented =candidate/nonmembership.ss=; it is not produced by the
positive Why DAG.  The generator and checker each have a work budget.
The checker recomputes the inspected-program fingerprint and binds
snapshot identity/generation/digest, candidate digest and the native
complete/empty result.  It checks every supported rule instance against
=C= and the absence of the ground target.  A bounded run never returns
a complete certificate.

Temporal must additionally declare whether the source inventory covers
the intended question.  A closed relation snapshot may still omit a
document, retrieval result or observed event; the certificate then says
only that =q= is absent from the supplied finite model.  Budget exhaustion,
non-ground questions and unsupported constructs return =bounded= or
=unsupported=.  Negation, aggregation and arithmetic require separate
semantics before their absence can be treated in this way.

*** Formal-model division
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-why-not-formal-division
:END:

The ASCENT owner should prove the finite positive-rule closure argument
as an abstract Lean theorem, then state separately what its Scheme
verifier checks at runtime.  A successful Lean compilation proves the
theorem under the encoded assumptions; it does not prove that the Gerbil
interpreter implements the encoding.  ASCENT's Quint model should check
snapshot replacement, proof computation and publication order, so an
old-generation proof cannot be installed as the current answer and a
bounded or unsupported result cannot be promoted.  This is distinct
from the mathematical closure theorem.

POO Flow's Quint obligation begins at the *cross-system* boundary:
GQL/ASCENT result identity, evidence cut and source-completeness policy
must match the conclusion revision that consumes them.  Its current
=CandidateExchangeExplorer= checks only generation and declared-provider
completeness in a finite abstract model.  A proof-aware receipt adapter,
source-coverage check and runtime refinement test remain open.  Keeping
these claims separate avoids counting two abstract models as an
end-to-end conformance proof.

The focused Scheme test checks relation canonicalization, distinct
retrieval/use buckets, exact assessment binding, conflicting judgments,
source/claim-version consistency and cycle-safe potential impact.  Quint
explores all four sound-use configurations of the first fixture, and
reviewer-order/conflict paths in the second.  These are local
structural and finite-behavior receipts; they do not reproduce any paper's
benchmark or establish end-to-end research quality.

The next evaluation should freeze a search corpus and record, per question:
relevant evidence available, evidence retrieved, evidence actually used,
assessment verdict and basis, claim support, correction-induced frontier,
and final artifact quality.  Report retrieval gaps separately from
utilization gaps, as in the long-horizon search study.  Include short
answers, extended search and autonomous research as scenarios using the
same relation model; compare against human-adjudicated relevance and claim
support rather than treating the number of tool calls as success.
