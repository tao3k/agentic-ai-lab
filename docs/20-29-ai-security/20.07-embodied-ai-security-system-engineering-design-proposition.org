#+title: 20.07 Embodied AI Security System Engineering Design Proposition
#+startup: overview

* Status

- State: proposal
- Scope: embodied AI, humanoid robots, and other AI-enabled cyber-physical systems
- Owner: agentic-ai-lab AI security architecture
- Normative language: MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, and MAY are requirement keywords

* Problem statement

Embodied AI closes a perception--decision--action--feedback loop across models,
software, networks, embedded controllers, sensors, actuators, operators, and the
physical environment.  A local digital failure can therefore propagate into a
physical effect, a safety consequence, an operational interruption, or a human
responsibility transition.

A component vulnerability list, a model evaluation, a penetration-test report,
or a formal proof is useful but insufficient on its own.  The assurance subject
is the composed system: its declared intent, authority, effects, dependencies,
recovery behavior, evidence, and residual-risk acceptance.

* Normative design proposition

#+begin_quote
Embodied-AI security MUST be engineered as a compositional system property over
authority-governed effects and continuously qualified evidence.  agentic-ai-lab MUST
connect system declarations, cross-domain risk, executable control flow,
verification obligations, authority-bearing receipts, and empirical operating
feedback without allowing any one representation or provider to claim complete
assurance by itself.
#+end_quote

This proposition has five inseparable clauses.

** The security subject is the constrained system effect

A vulnerability, threat, failure, or model error is an initiating condition, not
the final security subject.  Analysis MUST continue through the behavior change
and the resulting digital, physical, safety, business, privacy, and human impact.

Every security-relevant effect SHOULD declare at least:

- the effect domain and target assets;
- the initiating authority and authorization context;
- preconditions, invariants, and prohibited outcomes;
- commit, cancellation, compensation, rollback, and recovery semantics;
- fencing or replay protection where authority can change;
- the evidence required to establish completion or safe non-completion.

The ordinary public API MUST expose these concepts through POO-native objects,
prototypes, slots, extension helpers, and functional combinators.  Raw alist
DSLs, ad hoc patch languages, and unbounded =(lambda (self super) ...)= hooks
MUST NOT become the normal security-engineering surface.

** A security conclusion is an authority-bearing declaration

An architecture fact, threat, risk rating, requirement, control, test result,
waiver, residual-risk decision, or operating observation MUST retain the
authority that issued or accepted it.  A declaration without identity,
provenance, scope, version, and applicable context MUST NOT silently participate
in a system-level closure claim.

Authority is not a generic metadata field.  The model MUST distinguish at least:

- declaration authority;
- implementation and execution authority;
- evidence-producing authority;
- verification or attestation authority;
- residual-risk acceptance authority;
- recovery, handoff, and emergency-intervention authority.

Authority changes MUST be explicit system transitions.  Handoff, revocation,
replacement, and recovery SHOULD have identity continuity, fencing, freshness,
and audit obligations rather than being represented only as workflow comments.

** The security digital thread is a computable typed relation graph

The digital thread MUST represent semantic relations, not merely hyperlinks
between documents.  Its minimum assurance chain is:

#+begin_example
System / IntendedUse / OperatingScenario
  -> Asset / Interface / TrustBoundary / Dependency
  -> Threat / Hazard / Failure / Assumption
  -> BehaviorChange / PhysicalAndBusinessConsequence
  -> SecurityGoal / SafetyConstraint
  -> Requirement / Control / Responsibility
  -> VerificationObligation / Test / Review / Proof
  -> Evidence / Receipt / OperatingObservation
  -> ResidualRisk / AcceptanceDecision
#+end_example

Relations MUST be typed sufficiently to distinguish derivation, dependency,
allocation, mitigation, verification, contradiction, supersession, acceptance,
and invalidation.  Composition MUST detect incompatible assumptions, conflicting
controls, missing guarantees, withdrawn contributions, and evidence gaps instead
of flattening them into a single aggregate score.

Cybersecurity and functional-safety declarations SHOULD share architecture,
scenario, effect, and consequence identities while retaining their distinct
authorities, acceptance criteria, and verification methods.

** System change drives invalidation and obligation regeneration

Assurance is not monotonic merely because artifacts accumulate.  A change to a
model, sensor, controller, network path, firmware image, OTA service, operating
environment, supplier, policy, responsibility assignment, or recovery procedure
MAY invalidate an earlier conclusion.

agentic-ai-lab MUST be able to project a change onto affected assumptions, risks,
requirements, controls, proofs, tests, receipts, and acceptance decisions.  The
result MUST distinguish:

- evidence that remains applicable;
- evidence that is stale, superseded, or outside its declared context;
- conclusions that can be recomposed without new evidence;
- verification obligations that must be regenerated;
- residual risks that require renewed human acceptance.

Freshness, lineage, checkpoints, rollback history, and current publication head
SHOULD be part of receipt acceptance wherever stale replay or split-view state
could produce an unsafe effect.

** AI is an analysis provider, not a risk-acceptance authority

AI MAY parse architecture, map external knowledge, propose threats, generate
scenarios, identify inconsistencies, suggest controls, and prioritize candidate
obligations.  Every such result MUST remain attributable to a provider, model,
input context, and generation version.

AI output MUST NOT by itself:

- authorize a physical or security-relevant effect;
- declare a control implemented or effective;
- discharge a verification obligation;
- accept residual risk;
- replace the accountable human or organizational authority.

Providers translate and propose.  Canonical composition, execution authority,
verification, and risk acceptance remain governed by the agentic-ai-lab system model
and its explicitly assigned authorities.

* agentic-ai-lab responsibility boundary

agentic-ai-lab is the compositional assurance control plane.  It SHOULD provide:

- POO-native declarations for systems, scenarios, assets, boundaries, effects,
  risks, controls, obligations, evidence, receipts, and authorities;
- functional composition and projection of local declarations into system views;
- conflict, withdrawal, dependency, and assume--guarantee analysis;
- explicit runtime handoff for effects, recovery, and evidence capture;
- traceable refinement from normative intent to executable checks;
- change-impact analysis and obligation regeneration;
- provider boundaries for external standards, threat intelligence, scanners,
  simulators, robot platforms, and AI-assisted analysis.

agentic-ai-lab is not, by itself:

- a robot operating system, motion planner, safety PLC, or real-time controller;
- a vulnerability scanner, SIEM, penetration-testing engine, or model evaluator;
- a replacement for domain experts, safety authorities, operators, or assessors;
- empirical proof that a particular robot is safe in a particular environment.

Those systems and authorities integrate through declarations, providers, effect
boundaries, executable traces, and qualified evidence.

* Four-authority-layer assurance obligations

No single layer MAY claim complete closure.

1. Normative Org defines the intended architecture, semantics, responsibilities,
   risks, acceptance criteria, and required evidence.
2. Formal Lean establishes logical closure and refinement properties under
   explicit assumptions.
3. Executable refinement demonstrates that the normative and formal semantics
   project into runnable composition, transition, trace, and receipt checks.
4. Empirical qualification supplies evidence from the actual robot, controller,
   network, deployment, operator process, attack exercise, fault injection, and
   operating environment.

Cross-layer mappings MUST identify which normative claim is formalized, which
formal object is executable, which executable observation is empirically
qualified, and which assumptions remain outside the evidence envelope.

Formal success MUST NOT be reported as runtime or empirical qualification.
Runtime replay MUST NOT be reported as physical-system qualification.  A test
receipt MUST NOT establish more authority, scope, or freshness than its producer
and context justify.

* Minimum acceptance criteria

This design proposition is usable for a concrete embodied-AI system only when:

1. intended use, operating scenarios, physical consequences, and system boundary
   are explicitly declared;
2. security-relevant effects and authority transitions have named owners;
3. threats, hazards, requirements, controls, obligations, evidence, and residual
   risks form a typed traceable chain;
4. composition exposes missing guarantees and conflicting assumptions;
5. a defined change set can invalidate stale conclusions and regenerate affected
   obligations;
6. AI-generated candidates remain provider-attributed and human-reviewable;
7. Org, Lean, executable refinement, and empirical qualification are reported as
   separate authority layers with explicit cross-layer mappings;
8. the final report names unresolved assumptions, evidence gaps, residual risks,
   and the authority responsible for accepting or closing each one.

* Next assurance owner

The cross-layer identity and obligation binding for the five normative clauses is
owned by [[file:20.06-four-authority-layer-scientific-assurance-cycle.org::*20.07 cross-layer claim binding][20.06 — 20.07 cross-layer claim binding]].  It assigns

No implementation, proof, provider output, or test receipt may infer closure from
this RFC alone.

* Design consequence

Security is therefore a first-class input to system composition, not a final
inspection stage.  agentic-ai-lab creates value when it makes risk-driven decisions
composable, effects governable, evidence challengeable, responsibility explicit,
and change continuously traceable from architecture to physical operation.
