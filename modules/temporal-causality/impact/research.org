* Five distinct claims
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dynamic-impact-claim-ladder
:END:

1. *Structural reachability* asks whether a graph contains a path.  The
   existing structural Impact receipt answers this only for its selected
   relation inventory and does not establish temporal or causal effects.
2. *Temporal possibility* asks whether a candidate path respects admitted
   time constraints within a finite hypothesis family.  The Temporal model
   receipt records possible, necessary, refuted or unknown relative to its
   assumptions and exploration bound.
3. *Trajectory contrast* computes the pointwise difference of two supplied
   outcome series.  [[file:../trajectory/model.org][Trajectory]] owns the series; =impact/= implements the
   contrast arithmetic on
   an aligned, possibly irregular grid.  It preserves subject, outcome, unit,
   admission-cut and valid-time-projection identities.  Its status is
   =descriptive-contrast= or =candidate-contrast=; causal admission is false.
4. *Interventional effect* compares outcomes under specified intervention
   policies in a model that justifies the intervention semantics.  It needs
   explicit intervention, outcome, graph/model, horizon, support and
   uncertainty identities.  This module has no such estimator or proof.
5. *Counterfactual effect* additionally conditions on the factual history
   and needs a defensible abduction or identification step.  A generated
   alternative path alone cannot discharge that obligation.

#+name: dynamic-impact-claim-gates
#+caption: Minimum evidence required before promoting a result to the next semantic level.
| Level | Required receipt | Current status |
|-------+------------------+----------------|
| Structural path | Bound graph, relation inventory and witness | Executable structural Impact only |
| Temporal possibility | Clock assumptions, family bound and explored frontier | Executable finite classifier and Quint fixtures |
| Trajectory contrast | Aligned clock grid, subject/outcome/unit and evidence scope | Executable exact arithmetic in =impact/= |
| Candidate intervention window | Onset and end on the supplied grid, observed baseline, pre-onset agreement status | Executable exact trapezoidal score and time mean; no causal admission |
| Interventional effect | Intervention model, estimand, support and uncertainty | Open |
| Counterfactual effect | Factual-history abduction plus intervention and prediction evidence | Open |

For series values =L(tᵢ)= and =R(tᵢ)= on the same ordered clock domain, the
implemented descriptive contrast is =Dᵢ=L(tᵢ)-R(tᵢ)= and the interval rate
contrast is =(Dᵢ-Dᵢ₋₁)/(tᵢ-tᵢ₋₁)= for =i>0=.  Coordinates and outcomes are
exact, times are strictly increasing, and no resampling is implicit.  A grid
mismatch fails closed.  The formula is an arithmetic identity, not causal
identification; the LMTP paper's equal-interval contrast does not validate
irregular-time interpolation or an EIF for this kernel.

For a candidate intervention with onset =a= and end =b= on that grid, the
window audit reports whether supplied candidate and observed baseline agree
strictly before =a=: =consistent=, =diverged=, or =unobserved= if there is no
earlier sample.  It computes a signed trapezoidal score
=Σᵢ (Dᵢ₋₁+Dᵢ)(tᵢ-tᵢ₋₁)/2= for intervals within =[a,b]= and the time mean
=score/(b-a)=.  Both endpoints must be sampled.  This finite-grid functional
is not a verified continuous-time integral: changing the sampling grid can
change the score.  The code neither fills gaps nor estimates an intervention
effect.  A matching supplied prefix is an
arithmetic check, not factual-history abduction or a no-confounding test.

** Declared multivariate scope and negative controls
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dynamic-impact-scope-audit
:END:

The POO =ImpactScope= declares a nonempty set of directly edited measurement
identities =T= and an optional disjoint set of protected negative-control
measurements =P=.  It binds intervention identity, subject, admission cut,
valid-time projection, clock domain, onset and end.  Both sets are canonical
and digest-bound.  The audit receives one candidate and one observed series
per supplied measurement; inventories must have identical unique identities
and include every declared =T ∪ P=.  Each candidate/observed pair is compared
on its own aligned grid, with both window endpoints sampled.  Different
measurements need not share a sampling grid.  Candidate and baseline each
have one scenario identity, and those identities must differ.

#+name: dynamic-impact-scope-laws
#+caption: Finite-grid obligations for the supplied inventory, not causal identification.
| Obligation | Executable condition | Failure or unknown result |
|------------+----------------------+---------------------------|
| Factual prefix | For each supplied measurement \(v\) and sampled \(t<a\), \(C_v(t)=B_v(t)\) | Difference gives =diverged=; no prefix sample gives =unobserved=. |
| Protected output | For each declared \(v\in P\) and sampled \(a\leq t\leq b\), \(C_v(t)=B_v(t)\) | Difference gives =diverged=; empty =P= gives =unobserved=. |
| Direct target | Each =v∈T= is present in both inventories | Absence rejects the audit; no nonzero change is required. |
| Evidence scope | All series share subject, cut, projection and clock domain with the declaration | Mismatch rejects the audit. |

The aggregate =status= is =violated= if either checked obligation diverges,
=unknown= if neither diverges but an obligation is unobserved, and
=consistent-with-declaration= otherwise.  It is conditional on the *supplied*
inventory and the caller's chosen =P=; it is not a completeness certificate.
In particular, a measurement outside =T= is not automatically protected:
causal descendants of a direct target may change.  The audit does not
establish that a caller-designated negative control is truly unaffected;
that requires a separate causal model.  This interpretation takes the
onset/scope idea from [[https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/17af43527227c5c96db0f8d4c6aadc4e-Paper-Conference.pdf][CLEF]] without adopting its
conditional-mean assumption or claiming to reproduce its editor.  The
=causal-impact-admitted?= flag remains false for every audit.

** Refresh after a Trajectory revision
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dynamic-impact-refresh
:END:

An =ImpactRefresh= receives the historical contrast and one
[[file:../trajectory/model.org::#temporal-trajectory-version-delta][TrajectoryDelta]] per side.  It replays both deltas and the historical
contrast, rejecting mismatched source digests.  It then takes the following
pure branch; no runtime action or causal-effect assertion follows.

#+name: dynamic-impact-refresh-actions
#+caption: Historical output is retained in every branch; the revised output is a separate POO receipt when available.
| Condition on revised series | Action | Revised contrast |
|-----------------------------+--------+------------------|
| Both deltas =unchanged= | =reused= | Historical contrast, with identical source digests |
| Cut, projection or other comparison scope differs between sides | =blocked=, =scope-mismatch= | None |
| Sample coordinates differ between sides | =blocked=, =grid-mismatch= | None |
| Compatible series with either version changed | =recomputed= | New contrast with a new identity |

An identity-only, cut-only or valid-time-projection-only change still takes
the =recomputed= branch, because the Impact receipt binds series identity
and evidence scope.  A blocked branch does not silently reuse an obsolete
contrast.  This implements a narrow dependency update: it does not emit
streaming retractions, maintain every downstream query, or claim complete
lineage.  Those broader mechanisms remain with Temporal truth maintenance.
The distinct AI-research question of how evidence is retrieved, used and
related to claims belongs to [[file:../evidence/model.org][the Evidence relation model]].

* Paper-grade validation protocol
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dynamic-impact-validation
:END:

The next research claim must name its level above and publish a table with
source version, data or simulator, time-axis semantics, graph assumptions,
intervention policy, target estimand, identification assumptions, split rule,
metrics, seeds, confidence procedure and runtime.  A method comparison must
use the same estimand and horizon; otherwise it is a question comparison.

The executable suite should include:

- Known-truth synthetic SCMs with confounding, switching/persistence and
  delayed interventions; evaluate bias, interval coverage and calibration
  against the declared intervention-specific target.
- Unequal sampling gaps and late/corrected evidence; verify that the same
  admission cut under a different valid-time query does not reuse a receipt.
- Dynamic graph changes and hidden-parent stress tests; report candidate-path
  recall separately from causal identification.
- Timing and scope negative controls: move intervention onset, change only an
  unaffected variable, and swap candidate labels.  A supposed causal effect
  must not be inferred from a mere generated-trajectory difference.
- Ablations for known versus learned causal DAG, support/positivity diagnostics,
  and generated versus observed trajectories.  Report failure and unknown
  cases, including incomplete inventory and unobserved counterfactuals.

Current local receipts are the exact-arithmetic tests in
=t/temporal-impact-test.ss= and the existing Temporal model/Quint/Lean
gates.  They establish only their stated local invariants.  Reproduction of
DoFlow, CLEF, HEDRA, LMTP or the treatment-persistence simulation is open.

** Executable arithmetic receipt

The following named Org Babel block is a reproducible local gate.  Its test
cases cover nonuniform intervals, constant-offset rate invariance, input
permutation, candidate modality, grid mismatch, forged series digests,
pre-onset divergence, off-grid intervention onset, protected-variable
divergence, missing declared measurements, a changed source version,
unchanged reuse and blocked scope/grid refresh.
The command evaluates source code under the repository's heap-fenced Gerbil
entrypoint; it does not execute any paper's training or estimation method.
