* Abstract

Temporal Causality is a domain-neutral Agentic AI Lab Module for asking what is
known, possible, necessary *relative to declared assumptions*, refuted, or
still unknown about events and their causal explanations over time.  The
framework must support partial order, multiple clock roles, intervals,
late/corrected evidence, open candidate hypotheses, bounded exploration and
formal assurance without turning time order into actual causation.  Scheme
owns the POO semantic model and pure queries; Quint checks finite behaviors;
Lean can prove reusable semantic and projection laws; GQL and ASCENT can
propose structural candidates.  A vertical Lambda supplies its own vocabulary
and authority rules.

This is a research and implementation contract.  The status table below
distinguishes executable slices from proof obligations; the checked finite
model does not constitute the whole framework.

* Semantic questions

The framework must admit questions of the following forms without changing
the public type surface for each domain:

1. Which observations are committed before an explicit cut, and which future
   events are only planned, declared, or counterfactual?
2. What temporal relations are established, contradicted, incomparable, or
   unresolved across clock domains and incomplete evidence?
3. Which causal hypotheses remain possible under explicit structural and
   temporal constraints?  Which observation would distinguish them?
4. How do late events, corrections, retractions and watermark changes alter
   earlier conclusions while preserving the earlier receipt's identity?
5. What finite behaviors violate safety or reachability obligations, and
   under which fairness assumptions is progress established?

These are separate query families.  A one-number exploration cap, one
prewritten transition graph, or one healthcare action vocabulary cannot
define their semantics.

* Temporal type algebra

All public values are Gerbil POO objects or pure functional compositions of
them.  No raw alist or macro DSL is the normal user interface.  Each value has
an identity suitable for immutable evidence and replay.

The type relations are intentionally partial.  For instants =a= and =b=,
=compare(a,b)= is =incomparable= if their domains differ without conversion
evidence; it is =unknown= if the relevant coordinate is merely declared;
otherwise it is the ordinary coordinate order.  The distinction prevents
missing data from being mistaken for concurrent events.  Interval membership
returns the same uncertainty states and respects open or closed endpoints.
An elapsed duration requires monotonic clocks and cannot be inferred from a
wall-clock or logical-version coordinate alone.

For two proper observed intervals on the same domain,
=agentic-ai-lab-temporal-interval-extent-relation= classifies their ordered endpoints
into [[https://cse.unl.edu/~choueiry/Documents/Allen-CACM1983.pdf][Allen's thirteen basic relations]].
The relation concerns interval *extents*: endpoint openness still governs
membership, and no relation is inferred for a point or empty extent.  Those
return =degenerate=; unobserved endpoints return =unknown= and distinct clock
domains return =incomparable=.  The focused test enumerates all thirteen
relations and these three fail-closed states.  Constraint propagation over
networks of interval relations remains unimplemented.

** Bounded time observation

An immutable =AgenticAILabTemporalBoundedObservation= records a source, clock role,
observed lower and upper instants, precision, acquisition instant, receipt,
optional attestation identity, attempt and schema.  Its semantic digest covers
the complete field tuple, including each instant's domain, coordinate,
provenance and modality.  Replay recomputes that digest before a comparison.
The constructor validates structure but cannot authenticate a clock source,
receipt or attestation; those remain source-owner obligations.

For an observation interval =[l,u]= and observed boundary =b= on the same
domain, the pure comparison is =before= iff =u < b=, =after= iff =l > b=,
=equal= iff =l = u = b=, and =unknown= otherwise.  A different domain is
=incomparable=.  For two observations, =before= requires =u_1 < l_2= and
=after= requires =l_1 > u_2=; overlapping intervals remain =unknown= unless
both are the same point.  This rule never selects a favorable endpoint to
assert a policy decision.  The focused =check-temporal-time= gate covers
crossing, disjoint, invalid bounds and distinct domains.  The layer does not
yet turn this structural judgment into a policy proof, watermark closure or
duration budget.

For two bounded observations on the same monotonic clock, a numeric duration
is emitted only when the earliest possible end is at or after the latest
possible start.  Its lower bound is =end.lower - start.upper= and upper bound
is =end.upper - start.lower=.  Overlapping ranges return =unknown=, a fully
earlier end returns =reversed=, and distinct domains or unsupported clock
roles are explicit.  The POO duration assessment retains both observation
digests; replay recomputes its status and bounds.  This is elapsed coordinate
arithmetic, not a policy deadline, clock attestation or runtime budget spend.

** Source watermark claim

A =AgenticAILabTemporalWatermark= binds source, partition, inclusive sequence range,
bounded event-time observation, issuer, coverage receipt identity, late-event
policy identity and receipt identity.  Its digest covers every field and the
observation digest.  For a cut boundary =b= in the watermark domain, an
in-scope query yields =coverage-claimed= only if the watermark's *lower*
possible coordinate is at least =b=.  If its upper coordinate is below =b=,
the result is =not-covered=; a crossing interval is =unknown=.  Out-of-scope
sequences and partitions remain =outside-scope=, and clock-domain mismatch is
=incomparable=.  This is a pure structural assertion.  The source owner must
authenticate the named coverage receipt and enforce the late-event policy
before a runtime closes or revises an immutable causal cut.

* Model and query separation

A =TemporalModel= denotes an immutable evidence snapshot, candidate universe,
constraints and assumptions.  It does not contain a Quint work budget.  A query
selects a target and may request an operational exploration limit.  =#f=
means exhaust the *finite declared family*.  A positive limit permits an
unexplored frontier; it must not silently upgrade a result to =necessary=.
The old =MaxWorlds= name was misleading: the finite example selects candidate
identities, not complete possible worlds or counterfactual histories.  The
Quint configuration now calls this =ExplorationLimit= and treats it as a
checking scope: =0= derives the effective bound from the candidate set,
while a positive number requests an early stop.  Full possible-world semantics requires a separate behavior
model with explicit interventions, environment actions and observation map.

Classification is relative to the declared hypothesis family.  The
=agentic-ai-lab-temporal-model= constructor declares exclusive explanations;
=agentic-ai-lab-temporal-overlapping-model= declares potentially coexisting
mechanisms.  Both use the same finite constraint checker, and the family mode
enters the model digest and classification receipt.  For target
=h=, =refuted= means a known constraint fails; =possible= means the candidate
passes known constraints; =unknown= means it has not been explored or its
evidence is insufficient.  =Necessary= is available only after exhaustive
inspection of a caller-declared complete exclusive family, with one admitted
candidate and no unknowns.  This is a restricted uniqueness judgment, not an
actual-cause theorem.  The model does not invent mechanisms without a
hypothesis vocabulary.  The Quint hypothesis exploration currently checks the
exclusive mode; an overlapping behavior-space projection remains open.

** Bitemporal evidence revision

The framework distinguishes a fact's *valid interval* from the *admission
instant* at which the system accepted a belief about that fact.  This follows
the valid-time/transaction-time distinction of Jensen and Snodgrass, but the
current Module calls the second axis admission time to avoid equating raw
network ingress with an admitted belief.  One journal declares an admission
clock domain; comparing its coordinates is a caller-supplied ordering
assumption, not a proof of clock-source trust.

A revision =r= contains identity, subject, operation, predecessor, admission
instant, optional valid interval and optional content identity.  An assertion
is a root.  A correction or retraction points to an earlier same-subject,
non-retracted predecessor.  A retraction has no valid interval or content.
The journal retains every revision.  No update overwrites earlier evidence.
Branches are admitted as facts about disagreement: at an as-of cut, two
visible maximal revisions for a subject yield =conflicted=, with no arbitrary
latest-value winner.  A single retraction head yields =retracted=.  A single
assertion/correction head is active only if its valid interval contains the
requested valid-time instant; declared endpoint evidence yields =uncertain=
and a different valid-time domain yields =incomparable=.

For journal =J= and admission cut =a=, define =V(J,a)= as revisions whose
admission coordinate is at or before =a=.  The *cut digest* binds =a= and
canonicalized =V=, while the journal digest binds all of =J=.  For valid-time
query =v=, the *projection digest* separately binds the cut digest, the
full =v= instant row (or its absence), and the active/retracted/conflicted/
outside/uncertain/incomparable result buckets.  The valid-time instant
digest uses the complete row, so reusing an identity with a different
coordinate cannot silently reuse a projection.  In notation, the semantic
keys are =C(J,a)=(a,V(J,a))= and
=P(J,a,v)=(C(J,a),v,Result(J,a,v))=.  SHA-256 encodes these keys in Scheme;
the Lean theorem concerns the structural key, not a proof of hash
injectivity.

Appending a revision after =a= changes the journal digest but preserves
both the earlier cut digest and the same valid-time projection digest.
Changing =v= can leave the cut digest unchanged while changing the
projection and its answer.  The regression test checks both cases.  The
revision layer alone does not locate derived conclusions.  The separate
=truth-maintenance/= slice schedules dependency-based reevaluation; runtime
selection and compensation still require evaluator provenance and authority.

* Cut-bound reverse dependency planning

A =TemporalDerivation= is a POO object with immutable conclusion identity,
source cut and valid-time projection digests, policy identity, subject-premise
identities and conclusion-premise identities.  A =TemporalDependencyIndex=
binds both source identities,
validates unique conclusion identities and resolvable conclusion premises,
canonicalizes entry order, and computes a semantic digest from all declared
edges and the caller's completeness assertion.  The index's completeness is
*relative to an explicitly supplied derivation inventory*.  No algorithm can
infer that the caller supplied every hidden derivation or every policy
dependency; absence of an edge is not proof of independence.

For changed subject set =S=, let =D(S)= be conclusions with a direct subject
edge from =S=.  Let =succ(X)= be conclusions whose declared conclusion premise
is in =X=.  The affected set is the least fixed point
=A = μX.(D(S) ∪ succ(X))=.  The Scheme implementation builds reverse indexes
in one pass and traverses each conclusion once.  Dependency cycles therefore
terminate.  Its sorted result is independent of derivation order.  The plan
records the old and revised cut and projection digests, changed subjects,
index digest,
affected conclusion identities and an identical reevaluation schedule.  It
does not mutate historical conclusions, claim that reevaluation succeeded,
publish an active winner, or execute an effect.

=scoped-complete= means the caller asserted a complete derivation inventory
for this cut and dependency class.  =partial= means affected conclusions are
a lower bound; an empty affected set is not an unaffectedness certificate.
The lower-level planner accepts an explicit changed-subject assertion.  The
=plan-from-cuts= entrypoint instead accepts one journal, two admission
instants and one shared valid-time instant.  It replays both snapshots from
the journal, binds the index to the replayed prior cut *and projection*, and
computes changed subjects from per-subject visible revision identities.  It
marks a newly visible branch even if selected head or conflict status is
unchanged.  =plan-from-projections= similarly accepts one admission instant
and two valid-time instants and replays both projections itself.  Neither
public entrypoint accepts a caller-supplied snapshot as evidence.  Each
as-of query reconstructs the canonical journal and checks its digest against
the revisions before projection.  This is an in-process replay check; it
does not authenticate externally supplied data or prove a signed receipt.
Time-bound, watermark,
absence, resource and policy dependencies need their own declared premise
keys before they may use this index for complete invalidation.

* Valid-time reprojection

The two query dimensions need different invalidation rules.  For a fixed
valid-time query, an admission-cut advance uses changed visible subjects and
their reverse dependency closure.  For a fixed admission cut and a changed
valid-time query, =plan-from-projections= schedules *every indexed
derivation*.  The current derivation contract does not attest that any proof
is independent of the valid-time parameter.  Equal selected revision
buckets therefore do not make an old proof's projection key reusable.

The plan's =trigger= distinguishes =premise-delta= from
=valid-time-reprojection=.  An empty changed-subject list in the latter
means that the cause was query scope, not that the projections are
equivalent.  A complete schedule remains relative to the declared inventory;
an incomplete inventory gives only a lower bound.  In notation, for an index
=I= bound to =P(J,a,v₁)= and =v₁ ≠ v₂=, this slice sets
=Affected(I,v₁→v₂)= to every identity in =I=.  The plan neither infers a
new conclusion value nor publishes an active pointer.  Selective reuse would
require a checked time-parameter independence contract for each derivation,
including policy and absence premises.

Jensen and Snodgrass's bitemporal distinction
(https://www.cs.arizona.edu/~rts/pubs/TRmerged.pdf) motivates keeping
admission and valid-time axes separate.  Full-inventory invalidation is this
project's conservative proof-scope rule, not a claim made by that source.

The Quint submodel in =packages/proofs/quint/TemporalInvalidation.qnt= checks a finite
subject/conclusion graph.  =Direct= is the changed-subject frontier, =Step=
adds one-hop conclusion dependents, and =Closure= applies =Step= at most
=|Conclusions|= times.  The explorer schedules any enabled dependent, so
its action relation admits multiple traversal orders.  Quint checks that every
scheduled conclusion belongs to =Closure= and that terminal states equal
=Closure=.  Under weak fairness of =Advance=, it checks eventual completion
for the finite fixture with a branch, a cycle and one unaffected conclusion.
This is bounded model checking of the abstract graph, not a refinement proof
of the Scheme hash-table implementation.

The independent Lean 4.34 target in =packages/proofs/lean-poo/= uses
[[https://github.com/tao3k/lean-poo][LeanPoo.Proof]] =ProofObject=, =Patch=,
=pending= and =closePending=.  It proves that an old obligation with a
declared changed key enters the reevaluation frontier, that an unaffected old
obligation is excluded unless explicitly reintroduced, and that discharging
the selected frontier yields a revised certificate while LeanPoo reuses
unaffected obligations.  This proof is about a typed POO proof object; a
future projection theorem must connect concrete Scheme index rows and patch
footprints to those Lean obligations.  The target uses a separate Lean
toolchain because the existing Cedar-bound Agentic AI Lab proof package uses Lean
4.31 and LeanPoo currently uses Lean 4.34.

This design follows the explicit justification dependency idea of
[[https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/79-1/Papers/004.pdf][Doyle's
truth-maintenance system]] and the affected-fact propagation principle in
[[https://doi.org/10.1145/170036.170066][DRed]].  The current implementation
is a reevaluation scheduler, not a DRed algorithm for recursive deletions or
a complete justification-based belief revision system.

* Conclusion revisions and active selection

The =conclusions/= layer separates three values.  A
=TemporalConclusionRevision= binds a subject/scope, cut, projection,
policy/generation,
result or retraction, proof identity and predecessor.  A
=TemporalConclusionJournal= is a canonical immutable inventory that rejects
duplicate IDs, missing or cyclic supersession, cross-subject edges and a
change that reuses both the predecessor's cut and projection.  Two corrections of one predecessor
remain distinct branches.  A retraction is a tombstone revision with a proof
identity; it does not delete the prior result.  The journal's digest binds
all rows, so input order cannot select a winner.  The =journal-extend=
function constructs a new journal with every old row retained; the prior
journal remains a replayable value.

The change constructor requires an old conclusion in a =scoped-complete=
invalidation plan's affected set, exact old/new cut and projection binding,
and a new opaque
proof identity.  The reverse index proves only *which conclusions require
reevaluation*.  The supplied proof identity is a reference to evaluator-owned
evidence, not a theorem checked by this constructor.  Monotonicity, closed
absence, SCC stabilization and retraction validity must be admitted before
an external runtime treats the revision as publishable.

An active-pointer observation is =(subject, scope, version, selected,
observation-id)=.  =selection-prepare= checks that the proposed revision is
exactly the journal row, then compares the observation's version and selected
revision with the caller's expected version and the proposed revision's
predecessor.  A match produces a =cas-ready= plan with version =v+1=;
otherwise it produces =conflict= with no proposed version.  Both outcomes
are inert POO values.  A runtime must recheck capability and perform an
atomic compare-and-swap against its current pointer.  A plan prepared from
an observation can lose a race before commit.  After retraction, the pointer
may select the tombstone revision; current applicability must interpret its
=retract= operation as no active result.

The finite Quint model in =packages/proofs/quint/TemporalConclusionSelection.qnt= represents
two proposals against the same predecessor and expected version.  Its
abstract atomic =Commit= increments the version; the other proposal then
enters =Conflict=.  Quint checks at-most-one commit, version accounting,
selection membership and completed conflict classification across both
proposal orders.  Weak fairness of =ResolveAny= is an explicit model
assumption.  This does not prove a concrete runtime CAS implementation,
authorization check or effect compensation.  The Scheme test covers the
same bounded branch and stale-observation cases without claiming a general
Scheme/Quint refinement.  The separate LeanPoo 4.34 target proves for all
natural versions that, after one abstract version increment, a second
proposal against the old expected version is stale.  It does not prove that
the runtime's durable store implements that abstract atomic step.

[[https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/79-1/Papers/004.pdf][Doyle's
justification-based truth maintenance]] motivates retaining the reasons for
nonmonotone changes; [[https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(86)90080-9][de
Kleer's assumption-based TMS]] shows why alternative assumption contexts
need distinct identities.  This slice retains correction branches but does
not implement full assumption-set labeling or belief revision.

** Host-pinned finite-family conclusion admission

=admission/interface.ss= supplies POO source-snapshot and family-admission
values. It replays the registered domain/observation inventory, runs the
finite-family classifier and generates a proof digest and immutable root
conclusion bound to source cut, valid-time projection, policy and generation.
Unknown classifications cannot produce a conclusion. This is a proof of the
supported family-relative classification under declared hypotheses; it does
not establish an intervention-qualified causal fact or closed source absence.

The Runtime C source-registration control entrypoint maintains a bounded,
process-local registry. Ordinary semantic calls cannot register sources.
A higher host generation retires old snapshot applicability; a conflicting
registration at the same generation or a rollback is rejected. Python uses
the same control entrypoint on the owner thread. The trust premise is explicit
host registration, not externally authenticated signatures. External issuer
verification, complete partition/sequence coverage, correction/retraction
admission and authorized durable pointer commit remain open.

* Formal programme

The current =packages/proofs/quint/model.org= gives one finite instance, state machine,
invariants, liveness assumption and Quint commands.  The general obligations
are broader:

- *Temporal soundness*: every admitted native comparison follows from
  observed coordinates in one domain or validated conversion evidence.
- *Bounded-time arithmetic*: =AgenticAILabProof.PooC4.TemporalBoundedTime= proves
  that a definite-before or definite-after judgment holds for every coordinate
  inside the stated interval, a crossing boundary permits neither definite
  judgment, and two overlapping bounded observations cannot have a definite
  before/after order.  It also proves that pairwise definite order holds for
  every coordinate selected from each interval, and that a watermark lower
  bound at or beyond a cut bounds every possible watermark coordinate.
  This is an abstract numeric proof, not
  Scheme refinement or source receipt authentication.
- *Uncertainty preservation*: absent, declared, retracted or incomparable
  evidence cannot become a positive causal or authority claim.
- *Cut monotonicity under append-only evidence*: an unchanged evidence prefix
  yields the same as-of cut.  Correction/retraction needs revision semantics,
  so this law does not apply without an unchanged prefix assumption.
- *Future-append replay*: for an admission cut =c=, appending only revisions
  with admission position greater than =c= preserves the visible list at =c=.
  =AgenticAILabProof.PooC4.TemporalEvidenceRevision= proves this abstract list law
  and preservation of a fixed valid-time projection key; it also proves that
  distinct valid-time parameters give distinct structural projection keys.
  The Scheme test checks digest behavior for concrete instances.  Neither is a general
  implementation refinement proof.
- *Model/query separation*: changing a checking budget changes only the
  explored frontier and perhaps the answer's certainty, not the model digest.
- *Source correspondence*: formal instances and native POO values need a
  named interpretation; a general translation theorem remains open.
- *Behavior refinement*: a future observable map relates native state changes
  to Quint traces, including stutter steps and environment assumptions.
- *Proof/receipt binding*: Lean theorems, Quint executions and native runtime
  receipts retain their own identities and scopes.  One is not substituted
  for another.

Finite Quint state exploration checks an exact source inventory and finite
scope. The shared gate snapshots all local =.qnt= modules, checks that their
identities remain unchanged and binds the selected predicates, fairness
obligations, tool versions, exit, counts and output digest in a Scheme v1
receipt. A zero status without explicit completion and an empty queue fails
admission. Counterexample cases select a named predicate and must fail by
violating it; parser errors, process crashes and timeouts do not qualify.

=just check-temporal-family-quint= covers the full family, a one-step bound,
discriminating evidence and the incorrect-necessity mutation. These checks
are independent of the native POO classifier and its direct Scheme tests.
No general source-to-POO translation or implementation refinement theorem is
claimed. Such correspondence needs an explicit interpretation over observable
states, stutter, input assumptions and terminal outcomes.

* Work remaining for a complete framework

The [[file:design-audit.org][2026-10-04 design decisions and acceptance ledger]] settles the
extension rules for these gaps, including scoped evidence admission, frozen
Quint dependencies and authorized runtime publication. The ledger distinguishes
accepted design from executable closure and is the delivery-order reference.

1. Extend the bounded observation layer with source-verified clock
   conversions and authenticated watermark/completeness semantics over the
   existing event/ingress/processing axes, distributed duration budgets,
   interval constraint propagation, and partial-order constraints.  Align it
   with RFC 45-70 and 45-72.
2. Bind reevaluation proof admission to immutable conclusion revisions and
   an authorized runtime active-pointer transition under RFC 45-73.  The
   evidence journal, as-of cuts, reverse index, conclusion journal and pure
   CAS plan are executable; proof admission, signed observations, atomic
   publication and effect receipts remain open.
3. Extend the now-explicit overlapping hypothesis-family mode with actual
   interventions and behavior spaces.  Keep candidate generation, assumption
   admission and causal attribution distinct.
4. Define property and query objects for safety, reachability and progress;
   generate or interpret Quint transitions from the semantic model rather than
   exposing a caller-authored =Next= API.
5. Extend the Quint verification receipt from its staged source-inventory binding
   to a proved or differentially checked native/Quint projection.  The receipt
   currently binds Quint's own finite run, not the POO state-transition
   interpretation.
6. Extend Lean proofs from event/cut facts to generic temporal relations,
   source projection and trace refinement.  The abstract future-append cut
   lemma is now in the Temporal proof target; retain bounded Quint as separate
   executable evidence.

The POO [[file:trajectory/model.org][Trajectory module]] owns canonical, exact outcome series and
their time and evidence identities.  The separate =impact/= module consumes
two comparable series.  It computes pointwise level and interval-rate
differences, and a candidate intervention-window audit checks pre-onset
agreement and computes a trapezoidal score on the existing grid.
An =ImpactScope= additionally binds direct targets and explicitly protected
negative-control measurements across a supplied multivariate inventory;
its audit reports =violated=, =unknown= or
=consistent-with-declaration= relative to that inventory.
Trajectory now produces an immutable coordinate delta between versions.
Impact replays both source deltas and the historical contrast, then reuses,
recomputes or blocks a revised contrast according to source identity, evidence
scope and grid alignment.  The historical receipt remains available.
Neither module grants causal-impact admission.  The paper map, target
estimands, negative controls and reproducibility gates are in
[[file:impact/research.org][Dynamic Time, Trajectory, and Impact Research Contract]], especially
[[file:impact/research.org::#dynamic-impact-claim-ladder][the claim ladder]].

The 2026 DoFlow, CLEF, HEDRA, LMTP and treatment-persistence sources are also
mapped to repository owners in the named source table of the architecture
document.  None is currently reproduced by this module.  Future model

* Evidence relations across search and research
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: temporal-evidence-lineage
:END:

The engine also asks a distinct AI-facing question: which evidence was
found, which was used for each claim, which use has a separate assessment,
and which downstream artifacts may depend on a changed premise?  The
[[file:evidence/model.org][Evidence relation model]] owns this POO-native relation layer and
its finite Quint exploration.  It is shared by short answers, extended
search, long-running work and autonomous research.  None of those scenario
names is a public engine type.  Relation reachability gives a potential
invalidation frontier, not actual influence or claim truth.
