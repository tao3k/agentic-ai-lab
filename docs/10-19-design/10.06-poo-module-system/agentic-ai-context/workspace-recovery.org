* Interrupted workspace write: the design contract
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: interrupted-write-design
:END:

The workspace was changed, but the owner crashed before retaining the result
receipt. That is the scenario author's knowledge. The recovering Context does
not inherit that omniscient view: it retains the original Attempt and intended
effect, while the effect's completion remains unknown. A fresh file observation
can reveal changed bytes without establishing which action produced them.

This section and the =InterruptedWriteRecoveryDesign= view in
[[file:workspace-recovery.sysml][the existing SysML model]] define the current design discussion. They introduce
no implementation or prescribed action sequence. The historical execution
material below retains its original scope and does not validate this design.
The historical READY/repeated-boot case and the interrupted workspace write
are different cuts; owner readiness never proves workspace-effect completion.

The [[#recovery-dispatch-count-mvp][current replay-count design and figure]] explain how one scoped question
survives changes to evidence. The numbered summary examples are retained
as case fixtures.

** Evidence belongs to Temporal Causality

Durable consumes the existing [[file:../../../../modules/temporal-causality/framework.org][Temporal Causality framework]] and its
[[file:../../../../modules/temporal-causality/evidence/model.org][Evidence, Use, and Artifact Lineage contract]]. It does not own another Evidence
store, revision mechanism or causal inference language. Context relates those
objects to its tasks and exposes a scoped view. Durable supplies the domain
claims about an Attempt, effect completion and safe repetition.

Use the existing distinction between =EvidenceRetrieval=, =EvidenceUse= and
=EvidenceAssessment=. Finding a file or receipt records a candidate source.
Using it to support "this Attempt completed" binds exact source and claim
versions. An assessment records its assessor, method, verdict and basis for
that exact use. Neither retrieval, model confidence nor a supplied assessment
alone admits the claim. Admission must satisfy the applicable evidence policy.

Temporal revisions retain assertions, corrections and retractions. An as-of
admission cut answers what Context knew then; valid time answers when the
assertion applies. A late receipt can change today's assessment of the old
Attempt without rewriting what was known on the first recovery observation.
Time order alone does not establish a causal edge. Clock-domain uncertainty,
unknown validity and conflicting revision heads remain explicit.

** Objects and ownership in one scene

| Object or existing contract | Meaning here | Relations retained |
|----------------------------+--------------+--------------------|
| Original Attempt | The particular attempt interrupted by the crash | Task, original effect identity, saved composition, unresolved responsibility |
| Effect intent | The intended workspace modification and its scope | Target, operation and identity; separate from observations and results |
| Saved/current composition | Which module contracts and domain policies interpret this state | Core Feature binding and independent Durable provider; revisions remain visible |
| Temporal evidence lineage | File observations, receipt retrievals and their assessed uses | Source/claim digests, provenance, time, cut, assessment and correction dependencies |
| Causal hypotheses | Competing explanations of the observed change | Proposed cause/effect relations, constraints, supporting and refuting uses |
| Recovery judgment | A scoped conclusion about one question | Attempt/effect, premises, policy revision, Temporal cut and supersession |
| Reconciliation obligation | A question that remains materially unresolved | Target claim, derivation reason, responsible role, discharge evidence and dependencies |
| Exploration proposal | A model-selected way to distinguish explanations | Addressed obligation, expected distinctions, assumptions, cost and required authority |

The last three are Durable/Context design roles over Temporal objects, not new
Temporal evidence primitives. Core Feature assembles modules. Durable's POO
composition chooses outcome interpretation and retry policies. Neither layer
may bypass Temporal evidence admission or turn an exploration proposal into
permission to act.

** Unknown is scoped to a question

Keep three judgments separate: composition interpretability, original-effect
outcome, and eligibility for another dispatch. Each records its own reasons
and evidence cut. A missing receipt is an observation about the searched
inventory, not evidence of failure or of non-execution. Absence can support a
stronger conclusion only with an admitted source-coverage claim for the relevant
identity and interval. A timed-out search does not supply that coverage.

An outcome may remain unknown because attribution is missing, the source is
unavailable, the relevant cut is incomplete, clocks are incomparable, or uses
are contested. Preserve these reasons rather than collapsing them to a retry
Boolean. An unavailable or mismatched composition blocks interpretation under
that composition; it does not erase the original Attempt or raw evidence.
An admitted migration requires its own evidence and cannot silently relabel
an old claim as a current one.

** How evidence changes the same case

These are possible evidence changes, not ordered stages or mandatory branches.

| Newly available material | What it can change | What stays unresolved or prohibited |
|--------------------------+--------------------+--------------------------------------|
| File contains the intended text | Establish the observed content at the observed scope/time | Original writer and full effect completion remain unknown |
| A candidate result receipt names the effect | Add a source and a proposed use for the completion claim | Authenticity, exact identity and applicability still need assessment/admission |
| An admitted receipt establishes original-effect success | Revise outcome to succeeded and discharge its outcome obligation | Redispatch of that successful effect is vetoed; unrelated questions remain |
| An admitted scoped non-execution finding | Revise the corresponding outcome claim under its explicit coverage | Current permission and safe repetition remain independent requirements |
| A second writer could explain the same bytes | Retain an alternative causal hypothesis | Matching bytes cannot choose the original Attempt by themselves |
| A source correction or conflicting assessment arrives | Reevaluate dependent uses and conclusions at a new cut | Retain previous judgments as history; do not settle disagreement by vote |
| Permission expires while outcome evidence is unchanged | Reevaluate dispatch eligibility | Historical outcome evidence remains historical evidence |

Temporal truth maintenance identifies the potential affected frontier from
recorded uses and derivation edges. Reachability means "needs reevaluation",
not "the conclusion is now false". A partial dependency inventory cannot
certify that everything outside it is unaffected. Revised conclusions retain
predecessors and exact premises; an obsolete eligibility judgment cannot
remain currently applicable while those premises are unresolved.

** ASCENT derives responsibility, not a next step

ASCENT evaluates a finite, explicitly scoped Context snapshot containing the
Temporal cut, candidate hypotheses, assessed uses and Durable relationships.
Its derivations carry rule identity and premise identities. The following are
semantic rule obligations, not executable rules or a new tuple-based API.

| Premises in the scoped view | Derived responsibility | Discharge condition |
|----------------------------+------------------------+---------------------|
| Interrupted Attempt with unresolved effect outcome | Session/Task owner retains an outcome-reconciliation obligation | An admitted outcome for the exact effect under the applicable contract |
| Changed bytes are used for completion, but causal attribution is unsupported | A capable evidence investigator must examine attribution | Admitted evidence resolves that use, or the use is withdrawn while the parent outcome question remains open |
| Required evidence is unavailable or coverage is unestablished | Source owner/provider is asked for the scoped evidence or coverage | Admitted material resolves the gap; a failed retrieval only records an obstacle |
| Competing assessments or corrected premises affect a judgment | Configured evidence evaluator must reconsider the affected claim | A new admissible conclusion or an explicit still-unresolved result |
| Saved/current interpretation contracts do not match | Composition owner must explain compatibility or migration | Admitted compatibility/migration evidence for the retained state |
| A retry is proposed without applicable authority or safe-repeat support | Authority owner and effect-contract owner have separate checks | Both checks hold for the proposed scope; successful-effect veto still applies |

"Unsupported" and "unresolved" above are explicit scoped assessment statuses,
not unrestricted negation over an open world. An obligation identity binds the
Attempt, question and scope; repeated recovery retains it rather than adding
another copy. New reasons attach to that question. Distinct questions retain
distinct obligations. Responsibility identifies who can establish a fact; it
neither grants that actor access nor assumes a provider is available. An
unassigned or blocked obligation remains visible.

Closing an exploration task does not discharge its parent obligation. Evidence
correction can reopen a previously resolved question with a new revision while
retaining the earlier resolution. ASCENT's candidate derivations cannot by
themselves authenticate a receipt, admit an outcome or authorize an effect.

** The model explores within this evolving Context

The model sees unresolved questions, competing hypotheses, evidence lineage,
known access constraints and why each judgment currently holds. It may propose
inspecting a receipt source, comparing a scoped file history, asking a responsible
owner, seeking a discriminator for another-writer hypothesis, or waiting for an
unavailable source. These are examples, not a closed action vocabulary.

Each proposal explains which uncertainty it addresses and how different possible
findings would affect the hypotheses. The model can revise its explanation,
branch, pursue independent observations concurrently, or stop when no authorized
and useful observation is available. Budget exhaustion leaves an explicit open
frontier; it does not establish success, failure or safe retry.

Actual findings re-enter Temporal as source-bound observations and assessed
uses. Corrections propagate through existing dependencies. ASCENT derives the
resulting responsibilities over the revised Context, and the model chooses
again from the available exploration space. No rule prescribes a universal
"read file, fetch receipt, retry" order. Structural/causal relationships in the
SysML view express dependencies, not a state-machine workflow.

** Design boundaries and unresolved contracts

Completion admission needs an effect-specific definition: observing the desired
bytes may establish a postcondition while leaving historical attribution open.
A domain contract must explicitly say which claim is sufficient; this case
requires the original effect's outcome, not merely matching content. Hypotheses
are an open family and have no automatic exhaustive/exclusive assumption.

Safe repetition needs evidence covering the actual operation and target. A
"deduplication supported" label without its scope and guarantees is insufficient.
Current permission is checked independently. Even an eligible proposal is a
design judgment, not an execution capability. An unknown effect may remain
unknown indefinitely; eventual reconciliation requires sources that can answer
and authorized opportunities to inspect them.

The Temporal-to-ASCENT candidate exchange must bind the exact cut, source
versions and provider completeness claims. The existing Temporal evidence
contract describes that boundary as open. This design requires it and does
not claim an implemented adapter. Source authenticity, effect-specific
completion evidence, responsible-role resolution and scoped dependency
completeness must be supplied explicitly by the composed system.

* Inherited boundary and new obligation

This refines [[file:18-runtime-durable-source-commands.org][18: runtime durable source commands]]. That gate killed an initial
owner and recovered it. It did not establish behavior when recovery itself
dies repeatedly. The runtime protocol is unchanged in this slice; executable
runtime probes enter the existing replay and readiness commit boundaries.

The source command keeps its existing input identity and expected source cut.
Every new owner acquires a new durable epoch. Session identity and original
Attempt identity remain unchanged. Source applicability, current writer epoch,
historical completion and outstanding worker obligations remain separate axes.

* Readiness is current-epoch evidence

A committed old =owner-recovery:<epoch>= record proves a historical decision.
It does not certify the next owner or authorize opening before replay. A failed
old readiness input must not be relabeled as an applied decision. Each new
owner must complete its own reconstruction/replay and commit its own readiness
before its reactor can return bootstrap success.

Older failed readiness inputs remain historical bookkeeping in the retained
inbox. They are not pending source/authority commands and cannot grant effects.
Their cleanup/archival is separate from worker reconciliation. Removing such
metadata cannot be used as evidence that an old worker terminated.

| Recovery cut | Committed source state | Next owner's duty |
|--------------+------------------------+-------------------|
| Before source decision commit, repeatedly | Original source cut and original pending input | Reconstruct subscribers and retry the original command |
| After source decision commit | Retired source, affected Context fences and obligations committed together | Retain that decision; do not rerun or overwrite it |
| Before readiness commit | Source decision may already be committed; old readiness did not succeed | Retain fences and commit the new owner's readiness |
| After readiness commit but before bootstrap returns | Historical ready decision exists for the dead owner | Acquire a new epoch and commit a fresh readiness decision |

No result is published in these failed boots. The final successful boot still
uses runtime Context/Session checks. Portable old worker data cannot recreate an
observation capability. A/B lose current applicability, while unrelated C keeps
its actual completed result. Source re-admission cannot restore the original
proof or the captured original parent version.

* Exact recovery obligations

Recovering a live Attempt creates one unresolved obligation. Recovering a
schedule that already contains that obligation must retain it once, not append
another copy. Historical completed Attempts acquire no new worker obligation.
Every injected runtime cut checks exact fixture obligation counts and at most
one terminal per A/B Attempt. SQLite independently retains original terminal
payload hashes through all failed recoveries and the final successful owner.

The counts in this fixture are not a universal one-obligation limit for every
Session. They follow from one original Attempt per participating Session. The
production Session reducer continues to own scheduling/reconciliation.

* Read the existing recovery case as an object change

This explanatory slice selects the existing after-commit/before-bootstrap-return
boundary. It does not run a new crash or test. The runtime composition described
in section 18 is a proposed ownership refinement; the historical observations
above are not evidence that the new Module composition is implemented.

The previous owner's READY record names the previous owner.
The previous owner stops; the next owner has a different durable identity.
The saved source decision survives; the same unresolved Attempt survives too.
The new owner rebuilds from saved state before committing its own READY record.
Ready to receive work does not mean that the old Attempt has completed.

These sentences explain why the objects change. The committed journal is not a
copy of a live process. Its old readiness is historical evidence, its source
decision is retained without repeating that decision, and its unresolved worker
responsibility belongs to the original Attempt rather than a newly duplicated
Task. The final owner is current-ready while that responsibility can remain open.
A changed owner does not prove that an external worker has stopped.

The model may investigate missing worker evidence or competing explanations
under a newly admitted Context. That inquiry is separate from reconstruction;
neither the explanatory arrows nor a Feature profile prescribe its next action.

The two teaching views use the existing SysML package: Module extension explains
who contributes what, while recovery explains what changes and what stays. Each
view is read as an actual exported image. Findings retain the image/source cut;
a blank issue list does not establish that a young reader understood the design.

** Research correspondence

[[https://research.ibm.com/publications/reliable-actors-with-retry-orchestration][Reliable Actors with Retry Orchestration (PLDI 2023)]]
distinguishes completed invocations from invocations eligible for retry and
preserves call ordering across failures. For this design, a missing or
incompatible composition binding is a separate recovery condition. It leaves
the Attempt unresolved until the runtime can interpret its retained state.

[[https://www.vldb.org/cidrdb/2026/consistency-and-correctness-in-data-oriented-workflow-systems.html][Consistency and Correctness in Data-Oriented Workflow Systems (CIDR 2026)]]
provides a comparison for transaction boundaries and compensation. Our saved
binding identifies the rules and provider that can interpret the checkpoint.
Effect completion and compensation still require their own evidence.

** Three judgments on the original Attempt

Use the same cut after the old owner's readiness commit and before startup
returns. Consider a design reading where Core finds the original binding, but
the recovered evidence lacks a trusted result for an external effect. This is
an illustrative evidence combination within the existing case.

The original Attempt remains open across the owner change.
Matching the saved binding lets Core interpret the checkpoint.
Without a trusted outcome, the external effect remains unknown.
Retry requires current permission and protection against repeating the effect.

- Composition: compare the saved Feature revision, options, provider, contract
  version and checkpoint schema with the current binding. A mismatch keeps
  interpretation unresolved until an admitted binding or migration is available.
- Effect: inspect the trusted outcome tied to the original effect identity.
  A missing result leaves completion unknown. Historical READY and committed
  source decisions cannot establish an external outcome.
- Retry: ask whether present authority permits another dispatch and whether
  reconciliation or an effect-specific deduplication guarantee makes repetition
  safe. In this example neither is established, so retain the original
  obligation and withhold redispatch. A known successful effect is consumed
  as evidence rather than repeated. Compensation is a distinct authorized
  effect with its own outcome.

These are independent questions about a shared evidence cut. A successful
binding is not a completion receipt or a dispatch grant. Evidence can arrive
in any order. The runtime admits an action only against the applicable current
evidence and authority. Models may propose observations or reconciliation
without a fixed recovery workflow.

Temporal Event History provides the comparison for using recorded Activity
results during replay. Reliable Actors with Retry Orchestration provides the
comparison for distinguishing completed calls from retries within its actor
protocol. The CIDR workflow paper provides the comparison for durable
transaction boundaries and compensation. None of those scoped guarantees
establishes the outcome of an unobserved external effect in this case.

** Two extension layers in the same recovery

Core binds the Context recovery Feature to the independent Durable Module.
The assembly supplies a POO configuration to the Durable engine.
Durable interprets effect evidence and judges retry under its own contracts.

The module assembly admits the Feature contribution and binds the provider.
The engine configuration carries an outcome interpreter and a retry policy.
These are domain extension points. An assembly contribution may supply their
values. A standalone Durable consumer can provide the same values directly.

In the existing readiness cut, the new owner recognizes the retained
composition. The configured interpreter has no trusted evidence for the
original effect, so its outcome remains unknown. The configured retry rule
lacks current permission and safe repetition evidence, so the Attempt stays
open without redispatch. An extension may propose a decision, while the
engine retains admission and the host retains effect authority.

The outcome interpreter reads admitted observations for the original effect.
An admitted recorded outcome takes precedence over an interpretation. With no
trusted result in this case, the interpreter leaves completion unknown.
Current permission answers whether another dispatch is allowed now.
Repeat safety answers whether another dispatch could duplicate the effect.
A retry proposal must satisfy both guards. Neither guard is derived from a
binding match, an outcome interpretation or the policy proposal. Eligibility
is an inert decision tied to its evidence cut. Dispatch still requires a
current host capability.

For this evidence cut, project the original Attempt and effect, matching
binding, empty outcome evidence, and absent current permission and repeat
safety into the Scheme and Lean decision. The result remains ineligible
even if the retry policy proposes retry. An admitted successful outcome
for the same effect vetoes redispatch even when both guards are true.
See [[file:18-runtime-durable-source-commands.org][the projection and host contract]]
and [[file:workspace-recovery.qnt][the Quint recovery model]].

** workspace recovery projection artifact

The [[file:../../../../dev/durable/emit-recovery-projection.ss][projection producer]]
imports the Durable public interface. It emits the admitted case input before
running the policy and reads the actual decision slots afterward. The local
numeric identities name the original Attempt, effect, policy revision and cut.
Lean elaborates the emitted definitions and checks their projection. In this
case the policy proposes retry, but the engine withholds eligibility because
neither current permission nor repeat safety is established. The artifact
exercises a pure decision and grants no host capability.

** Host admission of a recovery decision

The Context runtime service accepts a live scoped reader through
=context-service-assess-workspace!=. The reader stays in the trusted
embedding. The durable inbox receives only the assessment identity, original
effect identity and expected owner epoch. In the owner transaction, the
adapter checks the retained workspace intent, current epoch and original
Session obligation, then captures a new observation with the live reader.

The embedding supplies a revisioned Durable policy and admitted composition
when constructing the adapter. The original handoff saves their portable
identity. Historical handoffs without that identity cannot satisfy the
binding check. The replacement owner binds its assessment cut to the current
epoch, composition, observation, Session revision and execution receipt.
Only a qualified original execution receipt supplies a recorded success.
A coherent workspace read supplies effect evidence and leaves historical
attribution to the outcome contract. It supplies neither current write
permission nor repeat safety.

The same transaction commits the observed state and Scheme decision to the
existing journal. The service reads that exact decision after commit. An
identical historical command returns the retained decision and a conflicting
command identity is refused by the existing inbox verification. A decision
receipt is inert. Positive dispatch requires the separate current authority,
reconciliation and capability consumption boundary.

The trusted service also exposes
=context-service-workspace-recovery-projection=. It reads the exact applied
assessment under the writer fence and compares the original Attempt, owner,
composition, Session revision and generation, observation and receipt cut.
An optional expected digest pins the receipt bytes. An obsolete or substituted
receipt is ineligible for export through this current-evidence entry. The
returned packed value is still portable evidence. Save those exact bytes and
pass their path to =just emit-durable-recovery-projection= to produce a Lean
module carrying the receipt digest and checked Scheme decision projection.

The existing AuthorizedEffectToken and strict mediation code own token
validation and nonce transitions. The latter already distinguishes a spent
nonce with missing observation from a committed effect observation. Integration
must persist the spend before invoking the effect endpoint and keep the
obligation unresolved when that invocation lacks a qualified outcome. The
existing Context grant observation describes current applicability. It does
not itself grant external effect dispatch or establish repeat safety.

The trusted embedding can optionally install an effect host with the Durable
recovery configuration. Its =.retry-premises= operation supplies current
permission and repeat-safety evidence for the retained intent. Its
=.retry-authorization= operation must authenticate the issuer and return
the current AuthorizedEffectToken validation context for the exact retry
command. A read-only embedding supplies neither premise. The strict token
binds the current epoch, original Attempt and Session, and the complete retry
command digest. That command names the original effect and exact applied
recovery decision digest. The owner compares the exact command digest in
the binding and semantic root with the journal-derived value. The issuer
decision has its own digest. The owner checks consistency of that digest
between the binding and semantic root and reconstructs the root before
asking the issuer to validate the capability.
The owner passes the same POO request to issuer verification and
consumption at the dispatch boundary. It contains the exact retry
command, the original intent and the packed committed receipt with its
digest, so the embedding can project the Cedar proposal from the
owner-admitted values.
Before issuance, =context-service-workspace-authorization-request= exposes
that same request from the current journal cut, only for an eligible
decision. The Cedar provider projects it into its existing request object.
The intent digest covers the canonical retry command, and the handoff
payload also names the original intent digest. The deployment supplies the
Cedar subject and runtime roots. The projection leaves the execution root
unchanged because it has observed no execution. This step produces an
authorization request only.

The Cedar runtime authority can check the signed grant against the exact
reprojected proposal without spending it. The check and consume use the
same signature, issuance, expiry, revocation and runtime/effect binding
checks. A successful check leaves the grant unspent and cannot authorize
an endpoint invocation by itself.

=context-service-retry-workspace!= reserves a fresh local handoff only after
the existing owner admits the command and commits the strict nonce spend.
A second fenced owner transaction takes that handoff, checks the same token,
receipt, current premises and authorization, and calls
=.consume-retry-authorization!= against the live issuer. Only the returned
consumed handoff reaches the configured =.dispatch-retry!= endpoint. Issuer
refusal leaves the durable spend intact and the effect unresolved. The
endpoint owns fresh execution admission and
worker evidence, with the retry command and original intent supplied as
separate values. Returning from that endpoint is not a completed effect or
an original Attempt terminal. An old ACK cannot re-create the local handoff.
A crash after consumption leaves the spend committed and the effect
unresolved. A crash during endpoint acceptance also requires reconciliation.
The dispatch transaction has a named inbox command tied to the consumption
command. On takeover a pending dispatch remains an unresolved external
handoff. It can neither recreate the live handoff nor block startup as an
unidentified owner callback.

The packaged Cedar port supplies the live application binding for the
workspace recovery run below. The existing Cedar token projection and strict mediation
are reused at their respective authorization and nonce boundaries.

[[file:workspace-recovery.qnt][RecoveryEvidenceInquiry]] is a bounded knowledge-change abstraction alongside the

API. =revise= assumes externally admitted, exact-effect evidence. It abstracts
Temporal source verification, digests, assessment methods and dependency closure;
it does not reimplement or prove them. Conflict blocks current success selection.
Correction may reopen the question while the earlier success remains history.

[[file:workspace-recovery.lean][The Lean companion]] states and proves properties of that abstract judgment:
observations, proposals and candidate findings preserve the outcome; arbitrary
inquiry traces preserve an initially unknown outcome; correction reopens its
question; revision retains previous cuts; successful effects veto eligibility;
and eligibility requires both current permission and safe repetition. The
booleans represent qualified propositions at an explicit cut, not ordinary
user-controlled feature switches. List membership in Lean corresponds to set
membership in Quint; ordering and duplicate proposal entries are not semantic
claims. A mechanically checked cross-language refinement is not supplied.

| Status of the exact W1 use at C | Required basis | Decision |
|---------------------------------+----------------+----------|
| Affected | A recorded path from the corrected premise reaches the W1 use | Withdraw it from current support |
| Pending | No such path is recorded, and dependency coverage for this use or its binding is incomplete | Keep it out of current success support and derive a binding question |
| Independent for this correction | Exact effect binding and use are admitted at C, with adequate scoped lineage coverage and no path from the corrected premise | Retain W1 as support under the applicable conflict checks |

An independence claim is relative to the corrected premise, the exact use
version, its claim and the selected cut. Checking only a producer name,
a raw record hash or a missing graph edge cannot establish it. Reachability
identifies judgments whose basis must be reviewed. Reassessment can produce
the same verdict when another independent basis survives. Past verdicts
and records remain available at their original cuts.

At C in this case, no valid success support remains. Context records an
unknown outcome for the same effect. ASCENT reopens the original outcome
question for its Task owner, derives a separate W1 binding question for an
evidence custodian or scoped investigator, and flags the reached judgments
for review. The dependency path provides the reason for each request. The
model may inspect the crosswalk provenance, look for an authenticated direct
binding between the storage identifier and the Attempt, or seek a separately
admitted write record. A candidate investigation result can inform admission
through Temporal. Neither the candidate nor an ASCENT question authorizes
another write. These inquiries may be combined, deferred or run concurrently.

| =single-commit= | Exactly one commit for this effect in the recovery window | An independently scoped storage audit with complete coverage and one matching committed occurrence | Keep open until the audit proves the scoped count |
| =no-replay-dispatch= | No replay dispatch for this effect in the recovery window | An independently scoped dispatch audit covering all relevant dispatchers and recording zero matching replay dispatches | Keep open until the audit proves the scoped absence |

The recovery window is the declared interval of this case. The audits bind
its endpoints, the original Attempt and exact effect, and the identities
needed to reconcile occurrences across the relevant stores or dispatchers.
A complete storage count can coexist with a replay that never committed.
A dispatch audit and a storage audit therefore answer different questions.
Incomplete, unverified or conflicting audit material leaves the corresponding
claim unresolved in this design slice.

ASCENT derives one open review per unchecked claim key. The key is scoped
to the summary lineage, so replacing a citation can discharge the same
question without duplicating it. The model may inspect coverage, compare
audit identity mappings, prepare a scoped draft, or defer. Claims can be
verified in any order. Drafting or publishing a summary leaves claim
responsibilities unchanged.

The selected publication policy for this example allows a partial revision
containing the verified append claim. It carries every currently checked
claim as an assertion and names every other manifest claim as unresolved.
For example, with only the append claim checked, the partial revision
asserts that claim and lists the count and dispatch questions separately.
A full revision requires all three claims and can be admitted directly.
If an audit contradicts a historical claim, a revised claim or explicit
retraction requires its own assessment. The next section models a refuted historical claim and a scoped rewrite.

At selection, Temporal checks the candidate version and predecessor,
its current evidence cut and review revision, and the exact correspondence
between assertions and verified claim bases. It verifies the unresolved
inventory and the coverage label against the manifest. The checks are
conjunctive and depend on the facts available at selection. An assessment
change invalidates an older draft until it is re-evaluated. Admission appends
an immutable version with its own references. The historical B payload and
its citations remain queryable as of B. A partial version can satisfy
publication for its declared scope while the aggregate review remains open.
The aggregate review closes when all claim checks are satisfied and the
full current revision is admitted.

** Closing dispatch coverage for an exact replay count
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: recovery-dispatch-coverage
:END:

The fixture has two dispatchers, A and B. The authority attests the dispatcher
inventory for the original Attempt, exact append effect and half-open recovery
window. An admitted manifest fixes the required ranges A/early, A/late,
B/early and B/late. Its real counterpart includes dispatcher identities,
namespace and generation, and routing or ownership changes inside the window.
The observed log sources cannot define the expected inventory.

A qualified range binds that manifest scope and current evidence cut. Its
authorized issuer seals the range with the applicable opening and terminal
fences. It accounts for all sequence positions in the range, including a
certified empty range, and reconciles duplicate deliveries or conflicting
versions. A missing range, stale epoch, gap, unsealed end or unresolved
conflict keeps coverage open. The finite model represents each verified range
by one qualified slot receipt and uses a fixed authoritative manifest. An
empty event field represents verified absence inside a sealed range.

| View | What the evidence establishes | ASCENT and publication |
|------+-------------------------------+------------------------|
| A seen, B missing | A has an admitted replay event with ID replay-42. B has no qualified ranges yet. | Close the zero-replay fact review as refuted. Keep coverage and exact-count reviews open. A withdrawal revision may be admitted. |
| A and B covered | The four distinct required slots are sealed for the same scope and cut. The union contains one distinct matching dispatch ID. | Close the coverage review. Close the exact-count review on admission of its own assessment. A revision may assert exactly one replay. |

An admitted positive event is sufficient to refute zero. Exact cardinality
requires an admitted complete manifest, a unique qualified receipt for every
required range, and deduplication by authoritative dispatch identity. A
forwarded copy of replay-42 counts once. A distinct replay-43 makes the
exact-one claim fail even when coverage is complete. The storage commit count
remains a separate judgment.

ASCENT can ask for B's missing ranges, an authority-backed inventory, a
watermark or reconciliation evidence. The investigation and draft choices
remain independent where their premises allow. Once the coverage basis is
complete, assessment checks the exact replay claim against the distinct
event count. The summary draft binds that assessment, the manifest and
range receipts, the current cut and review revision, and its predecessor.
An older draft cannot silently inherit a new count.

The illustrated path admits a withdrawal revision v2 while coverage is open.
It then admits v3 with the exact-one claim after the separate assessment.
B and the prior v2 remain queryable with their own bases. A design can also
admit the exact revision directly when its complete basis is already available.

** Admitting exactly two replays after the corrected seal
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: recovery-dispatch-exact-two
:END:

B's new sealed range contains a record labeled R43. A's qualified range
contains R42. The labels in an export do not settle dispatch identity.
The dispatch authority binds each observation to a canonical dispatch
identity, scoped by authority, dispatcher namespace, incarnation and
dispatch sequence or an equivalent collision-resistant identity. A forwarded
copy or repeated observation retains the original identity. In this case
the authority establishes two distinct identities.

| Check | What must be verified |
|-------+-----------------------|
| Same subject | Both attestations bind the original durable Attempt, exact append effect and replay relation. A different Attempt or effect cannot contribute to this count. |
