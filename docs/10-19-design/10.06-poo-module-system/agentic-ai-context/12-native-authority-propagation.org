* Owning design and responsibilities

This extends [[file:11-native-eager-propagation.org][the source/support propagation slice]], following
=core/module-system/design/context-temporal-lifecycle-policy-system-design.org=,
especially its independent lifecycle axes and accept-result / invalidate /
reconcile transitions. Context coordinates admitted information and derived
working inputs. Session still owns its pure schedule, Attempt and obligation
reducers; Session does not import an authority graph or own a clock.

The use Host owns canonical immutable grant generations. The policy Host owns
canonical policy revisions and observed effective clocks. The native proof Host
retains original ASCENT derivations, support and append-only journals. Context
captures both original proof and exact grant/policy references in a version,
then captures parent VERSIONS rather than mutable Session heads. A later grant
or head cannot bless an older dependency. Revocation never edits source facts,
historical derivations, provider observations or already recorded terminals.

* Prepare before publication, push before reuse

The new internal =runtime/change-feed.ss= provides a serialized owner barrier:

1. Canonicalize and validate the proposed authority change and monotonicity.
2. Construct an event retaining both original and replacement owner values.
3. Ask every enrolled native POO observer to prepare its affected frontier.
4. If preparation fails, publish neither authority nor Context changes.
5. Install the pending barrier and publish the registered owner value.
6. Synchronously deliver prepared native batches. Context fences affected active
   Attempts into recovery obligations and materializes invalidation in cached
   Context views. Unaffected active Attempts keep their original revisions.
7. On delivery failure, leave the new authority and pending barrier in place.
   Block authority reads/final result admission until explicit owner drain.
   Retry against actual cancellation/recovery/terminal state; do not restore a
   saved prepared schedule. Repeated delivery is idempotent.

Default eager enrollment subscribes to use/policy owners before inserting the
first Context. Proof binding also subscribes to that proof's actual policy
owner before publishing its new input. Enabling propagation on an existing
owner visits retained parent versions as well as current heads. Current-use
checks remain additional fences; they are not the mechanism that materializes
normal invalidation. A cached =current= read performs no proof/use query.

Authority dependencies and source support are separate edges. A grant update
invalidates captures of its old generation. A policy revision or nonapplicable
window invalidates matching grant uses. Any policy-generation change fences
original proof captures expecting that generation. The latter is deliberately
conservative: this slice does not certify unaffected proofs across a clock-only
generation advance inside the same policy window. Such proofs require fresh
admission. Consumers using a different unchanged policy are unaffected unless
they depend on an affected Context version.

* What can be known in advance

A deadline is known at policy registration. =policy-host-deadlines= exposes
half-open window end tickets with policy identity and expected generation.
=policy-host-fire-deadline!= rejects an early ticket, a stale generation, a
foreign clock domain or an unobserved instant. A trusted owner scheduler supplies
the actual observed instant; firing routes through the same preflight and push
barrier as policy replacement. At the exact end coordinate the window is closed.

The native fixture proves ticket registration before expiry and actual expiry
push without querying any consumer. This is not an autonomous OS timer or a
wall-clock delivery-latency qualification. No process can predict an arbitrary
future withdrawal event. For an unannounced revocation, "before" means prepare
the dependent frontier before the new authority state becomes usable, with a
barrier covering partial delivery.

The reverse planner constructs subject/conclusion adjacency and expands each
visited conclusion once using hash sets and a queue. Its graph traversal is
expected O(V+E), including cycles; it does not search permutations of consumers.
This does not establish end-to-end linear time: canonical sorting, index
validation/digests, original proof replay, allocating receipts and rebuilding
this owner-local graph have separate costs. This slice makes no throughput or
latency claim and has no retained incremental-index or deadline-heap benchmark.
The substantive advantage demonstrated here is explicit dependency knowledge
and proactive fencing rather than waiting for a later retrieval to notice loss.

* Executable scenarios and native evidence

=12-authority-propagation.sysml= is an executed contract fixture, not a native
concurrency proof. It covers revoke, policy replacement and observed expiry,
early expiry, independent C, retained originals and four named unsafe modes:
query-only invalidation, blanket invalidation, recovery clearing the latch and
duplicate terminal publication. =run-authority-scenarios.py= requires actual
run-engine verdicts, retains failing traces and replays an unsafe/safe pair.

The Scheme fixture starts real A, B and C worker threads. A uses the changed
grant/policy and native original ASCENT proof. B uses an independent safe grant,
policy and proof but captures A's exact Context version. C uses B's safe
authority/proof with no dependency on A. Each native event must immediately
materialize invalidation in cached A/B views, remove their active schedules,
and leave C's original active revision and final result publication intact.

For EACH of grant revocation, policy replacement and deadline expiry, the native
corpus enumerates all 280 linear extensions of seven owner operations:

| Event | Actual native operation | Happens before |
|-------+-------------------------+----------------|
| 1 | A worker returns; real join creates opaque observation | 2 |
| 2 | capture current A revision for result proposal | 3 |
| 3 | A final result publication through current owner | |
| 4 | authority owner publishes and pushes invalidation | |
| 5 | A cancellation publishes an obligation | 6, 7 |
| 6 | actual join and reconciliation at cancellation revision | |
| 7 | A recovery publishes against actual current revision | |

After EVERY step, cached invalidation and at-most-one terminal are checked.
Historical successful completion may remain recorded but must lose current-use
eligibility after event 4. B's late result is refused and actual termination
resolves its pending obligation. C's exact original Attempt completes. This
is exhaustive only for this bounded operation alphabet and happens-before
relation; it is not every joint two-Attempt/provider/thread-machine schedule.
The native path is substantially more than completion intent or a finite model:
it executes original ASCENT derivation replay, support/policy/journal guards,
Context version propagation, actual worker observations and final publication.

Three native missing-subscription controls intentionally omit eager enrollment.
Their cached A/B remain admitted/active after each authority event, demonstrating
the defect in query-only handling. The independent final guard still denies the
late result; these controls do not claim successful stale publication. Three
fault cases reject preflight atomically, then fail delivery after owner publish,
verify admission is blocked, cancel/reconcile a real worker, drain, and verify
that the existing terminal and unaffected C survive.

Every native path emits commitments to its authority event, Context reverse
index, original journal and original ASCENT proof. Owner event histories retain
old and revised grants/policy snapshots in memory. Logs and path artifacts stay
outside source control. =run-native-authority.py= checks all Case/harness/final
markers and failure markers, exact path corpus, negative controls, artifact
hashes, source hashes and current declared/installed ASCENT and Orgize pins.
It refuses stale or tampered receipts. Source-owner and older owner-CAS tests
remain regression lanes in the same process (eight sampled paths each).

Native qualification results are recorded below only after the current source
cut passes; an interrupted build/test is not a passed receipt. These are
owner-local in-memory guarantees, not persistent crash recovery, distributed
CAS, provider revocation, authenticated sources, arbitrary negative-query
support completeness, task quality or comparative compression benchmarks.

The existing closed propagation receipt retains its legacy field names. For an
=authority-change= event, =source= is a namespaced authority identity, the
=cut/program= fields commit to old/new authority snapshots, and =proof-index=
is the authority event commitment, not a newly computed ASCENT source-proof
index. =context-index= is the actual native version/consumer dependency index.
The separately retained original proof and journal commitments identify the
ASCENT/support evidence; authority loss does not fabricate a source retraction.

The new exhaustive entrypoint has a 2700-second aggregate budget for three
280-path native corpora, with a 2520-second Case ceiling and real completed-path
progress. It does not change existing source/owner-CAS budgets or semantic
assertions. Earlier partial diagnostics, an interrupted 900-second corpus and
transient dependency-build failures remain unqualified local logs.

Cross-Session edges in this slice are explicit admitted =depend-on!= captures.
Automatic propagation traverses those captured edges and native original support;
it does not discover undeclared dependencies in arbitrary natural-language
memory, and it does not repair a missing admission edge after the fact.
